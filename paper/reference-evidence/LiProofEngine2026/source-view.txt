Abstract
Background: AI systems can produce proof sketches, formal code, solver artifacts,
and candidate strategies faster than research communities can assess them. Longrunning and parallel agents can also hallucinate, lose problem context, duplicate
obligations, or return incompatible formulations. Disclosure and local checking
alone do not show which claim has been earned, whether its dependency route is
closed, or whether evidence remains current after correction.
Methods: Proof Engine Infrastructure was developed as a fail-closed method
for claim-level research reporting. An agent-to-claim control plane externalizes
claims, obligations, and receipts in a typed directed hypergraph. Its minimum loop
declares the target and admissible roots, decomposes claim paths, dispatches work,
assigns edge-adequate evidence boundaries, binds checked inputs and outputs,
composes earned edges, and recomputes the frontier. A contrasting-case analysis
examined three completed projects with different mathematical objects and two
closure architectures. An odd-sum case visualizes parallel checks, asynchronous
returns, frontier selection, an unresolved continuation, and retained knowledge.
Results: A new uniform Hamilton classification tested paper-to-kernel binding. A
new exact all-N solution of Erdős Problem 848 completed the finite range left by
earlier GPT-5-assisted work, using compression, certificates, semantic checking,
complete coverage, and kernel replay. A rational-Dyck-path project repeated
direct closure on a new object. The running graph showed five parallel checks,
one selected open frontier, and retained partial and negative routes without
claiming closure. A separate large ongoing proof program provided qualitative

1 Background

42

AI-assisted mathematics is often framed as a question about model capability: can a

43

system prove a theorem, solve an olympiad problem, or translate an informal argument

44

into a proof assistant? These are important generation questions. They do not finish

45

the work of research. Once a plausible argument, formal file, certificate, or computation

46

exists, a project must determine what that object actually establishes and how far its

47

support may travel.

48

That post-generation task has five connected parts. First, the project must identify

49

the exact mathematical object being checked. Second, it must decide whether the

50

available check is adequate for the claim. Third, it must determine whether the checked

51

local pieces compose into the announced target. Fourth, it must preserve or withdraw

52

their status when definitions and dependencies change. Finally, it must keep the next

53

unresolved obligation visible while several people or agents work at once. These are not

54

separate administrative concerns. Together they determine the current research state.

55

A simple endpoint mismatch shows why local success is insufficient. Suppose a

56

formal file compiles, but a later reader discovers that its theorem uses a different

57

endpoint convention from the paper. The kernel result remains valid for the formal

58

statement it checked. The paper claim nevertheless loses its support, because the

2

checked statement is not the statement consumed by the argument. Rebuilding the same

59

file or refreshing its hash cannot repair that semantic break. Downstream conclusions

60

must be reconsidered until the binding is corrected and checked again.

61

Long-running and parallel agents create the same research-state problem at a larger

62

scale. A worker may forget a load-bearing constraint, hallucinate a bridge, or return

63

a proposition at the wrong scope. Several workers may duplicate one obligation, use

64

incompatible definitions, or advance branches that no longer serve the active target.

65

The difficulty is not merely that agents can make mistakes. It is that accepted facts,

66

tentative ideas, failed routes, definitions, and unresolved dependencies cannot remain

67

only in transient prompt histories if their outputs are expected to compose.

68

Existing approaches cover important parts of this chain. Model and benchmark

69

evaluations measure proof search, autoformalization, or task performance [36, 37, 39–41].

70

Agentic provers add planning, retrieval, parallel workers, repair, and formal feedback

71

[25–27]. Proof assistants answer a narrower question: does this formal artifact prove

72

this formal statement? Research-integrity proposals address another part. They use

73

structured disclosure and workflow documentation to make AI provenance and human

74

responsibility more visible [1, 2].

75

Each contribution is useful. None of them, by itself, records whether the checked

76

object matches the paper claim. None determines whether local results close the full

77

dependency route. None says which conclusions must be withdrawn after a correction.

78

These are reproducibility questions in the broad sense of keeping the scholarly record

79

accurate, inspectable, and self-correcting [3].

80

Proof Engine Infrastructure addresses this missing layer by representing the research

81

state explicitly. The exact target, its definitions, claim paths, work units, evidence

82

receipts, and active frontier are stored outside any individual agent context. Generated

83

outputs begin as candidates. A candidate can carry an inference only after an adequate

84

check is bound to the exact proposition and current semantic object. Typed graph

85

3

86

edges then record how earned claims compose; failed and superseded routes remain

87

auditable without contributing to closure. When a load-bearing object changes, affected

88

edges lose current reachability and must be earned again; a name or refreshed hash

89

carries no inherited status.

90

The persistent unit is therefore the claim. Workers are transient. Proof Engine

91

uses an agent-to-claim control plane : human and machine agents receive typed claim

92

obligations, while claims, contracts, receipts, and frontier state remain available after

93

those agents exit. It differs from a graph of agent communication or authority. Agents

94

may generate and exchange artifacts, but only a bound receipt may change a claim’s

95

inferential status.

96

Under its stated contracts and promotion gates, this architecture joins correctness

97

and coordination without confusing them. Independent obligations can be developed

98

concurrently because their required inputs and outputs are typed in advance. Asyn-

99

chronous returns can arrive in any order, but trust still enters through the same

100

checking, binding, and composition rules. The graph cannot prevent hallucination or

101

context loss inside a model. It keeps an unbound return from changing the mathemati-

102

cal status of the target. The same protection applies to stale evidence and duplicated

103

work. An incorrectly specified contract or mistaken binding remains possible; the

104

limitations below preserve that human-review boundary.

105

The method was shaped by two public projects containing new mathematics. It was

106

then reused successfully on a third object. The first project gives a uniform Hamilton

107

cycle and path classification. It tests whether a human argument and premise-free

108

Lean endpoints express the same result [15]. The second project gives an exact all-N

109

solution of Erdős Problem 848 [17]. It tests whether structural mathematics, exhaustive

110

certificates, semantic checking, and kernel replay compose into one closed theorem.

111

The rational-Dyck-path project reuses the direct mathematical-to-kernel pattern for a

4

counterexample and cover results [21]. A companion paper supplies the local verification-

112

profile concept [16]. This article develops the global state and transition method that

113

composes such records over time.

1.1 Contributions

115

1. An agent-to-claim control architecture. It makes claims and receipts persistent while

116

treating agents as replaceable workers. It decomposes an exact consumer into typed

117

claim paths that may be developed concurrently. Within a fixed root snapshot, only

118

evidence-bearing earned edges may change closure (Sections 2.3–2.7).

119

2. A temporal semantics. Immutable definition contracts identify the mathematical

120

subject. An admissible root registry prevents closure by declaration. Invalidation and

121

re-earning let later corrections withdraw inferential credit without erasing history

122

(Sections 2.5 and 2.8).

123

3. A research-control rule. The active frontier remains tied to the exact consumer.

124

Formal, certificate, witness, and replay boundaries are chosen edge by edge using

125

adequacy and total verification cost (Sections 2.10–2.12).

126

4. A contrasting-case comparison. It covers direct kernel closure, certificate-heavy

127

exact closure, and reuse on a third mathematical object. A running claim graph

128

shows earned, open, partial, and refuted routes. A separate stress case examines

129

concurrent development (Section 3).

1.2 Scope and non-goals

131

The subject of the article is research state: how generated material acquires, loses, and

132

regains inferential force. SAT solving, proof-assistant design, and certificate formats

133

enter only as possible checking boundaries. The article therefore publishes a method

134

comprising evidence identity, promotion and correction rules, frontier discipline, and

135

closure-respecting composition. Software distribution and implementation tutorials

136

5

137

lie outside its scope. Concrete deployments supply evidence and counterexamples,

138

while their commands, schemas, and project-specific source trees remain outside the

139

140

checker badges fails to instantiate the method. It must implement the closed-loop

141

claim, binding, promotion, correction, and frontier invariants stated below.

2.1 Method at a glance

144

145

146

147

148

149

150

151

which support has actually been earned.

2.2 Design and evidence base

196

The method was developed iteratively against completed work in one AI-assisted math-

197

ematical research program. The cases share that provenance, but they do not ask the
8

same mathematical question. One is a graph classification. One is an extremal number-

198

theory theorem solved for every N through structural mathematics and exhaustive

199

certificates. The third concerns order questions for rational Dyck paths.

200

The cases were selected to maximize contrast in mathematical object and evidentiary

201

architecture. They were not selected to estimate a population effect. Each included

202

case needed a stable record, a substantive machine-checking boundary, and a status

203

transition that tested part of the method. Two main regimes met those conditions: direct

204

constructive kernel closure and certificate-heavy exact closure. The rational-Dyck-path

205

project then tested the reuse of direct closure on a third object.

206

The unit of analysis was a claim edge and its status transition. The truth of one

207

theorem was not used to validate another. The evidence base consisted of public

208

mathematical manuscripts and formal packages for the successful projects. For each

209

case, the analysis recorded the intended subject, checking boundary, downstream

210

consumer, dependency route, correction behavior, and final publication label. The

211

comparison is qualitative and descriptive; no statistical analysis was used. Additional

212

file 1 provides the reporting checklist for these fields without exposing project-specific

213

commands, source trees, or private implementation details.

214

Separately, one large ongoing proof program served as a development stress case for

215

concurrency. It exercised parallel claim-path work in mathematical search, formalization,

216

independent review, correction, and final assembly. Both narrower and wider parallel

217

218

2.3 From generated artifact to reportable research claim

227

The loop begins with a candidate. An AI agent may propose a proof strategy or write

228

an encoder. It may also produce a proof sketch, translate a definition, search examples,

229

or summarize failed attempts. All of these activities belong to generation. Their outputs

230

acquire positive inferential force only after an appropriate verification boundary has

231

been crossed.

232

Claim 1 (Evidentiary status is output-relative). An output may carry inferential force

233

only through an independent verification procedure with stated scope. The identity of

234

the human or machine that proposed it supplies provenance, not inferential force.

235

236

The design objective is to support multiple fallible generation processes while
making their mathematical outputs inexpensive to verify independently.

237

The claim graph is therefore a shared control plane for concurrent research workers.

238

A single-agent transcript is a different object. A route is first decomposed at the

239

claim level. Separate agents may then develop its producers or attack an independent

240

route. They may instead search for a counterexample or construct a checker. Their

241

private search trajectories may differ. Their returns still meet the same typed consumer

242

contracts.

243

Definition 1 (Claim-path work unit). A claim-path work unit names a parent con-

244

sumer and the exact child proposition to be produced. It also records the input contract,

245

definition contract, admissible verification boundaries, and evidence fields required for

246

promotion. A worker may choose how to generate the candidate. It may not silently

247

redefine the contracted output.
10

Definition 2 (Agent-to-claim control plane). An agent-to-claim control plane maps

248

transient worker actions to persistent claim state. Agents receive claim-path work units

249

and may return artifacts, counterexamples, or proposed transports. No worker identity,

250

message, or handoff changes reachability. A state transition occurs only when the

251

returned object is checked, bound to its contracted claim, and admitted under the active

252

snapshot.

253

When these contracts and gates are enforced, this separation makes concurrency

254

safe at the research-state level. Agents can work on disjoint or competing paths without

255

sharing epistemic authority, and asynchronous returns can be evaluated in any order

256

against the active snapshot. The graph accepts a return through its evidence receipt

257

and binding, not through the identity, confidence, or persistence of the worker that

258

produced it.

259

Externalized state also changes failure handling. If a worker loses context, the con-

260

tracted proposition and definitions remain fixed outside that context. If it hallucinates

261

a lemma, the unbound return stays candidate. If two workers produce the same result,

262

both artifacts may remain auditable, but the shared edge is earned only once. If their

263

scopes differ, the graph records distinct claims instead of merging them through prose.

264

The same state can guide allocation. A dispatcher can avoid knowingly repeating

265

an already earned or retired work unit. It can run independent obligations in parallel.

266

It can delay a dependent unit until its inputs exist, and it can reuse a current receipt

267

across several consumers. These mechanisms supported useful concurrent progress

268

in ongoing development. They also define variables for a controlled comparison. The

269

existing record was not collected under a fixed latency, token, or throughput protocol

270

and cannot establish net acceleration.

271

In the running example, induction, pairing, and the shortcut are therefore recorded

272

first as generated routes instead of three competing degrees of proof. The engine may

273

dispatch the induction base, recurrence identity, algebraic closure, symmetric pairing,

274

11

275

and shortcut counterexample as five separate work units. Their checks can run in

276

parallel because the required outputs are typed in advance. Before their bound receipts

277

return and compose, none changes the status of T .

278

The work-unit mechanism separates responsibilities that are often collapsed in one

279

description of an AI system. Producing a candidate is one function. Checking it is

280

another. Composition and publication are different again. The same person or program

281

may perform several functions, but success in one supplies no automatic authority in

282

the next.

2.3.1 The generation-verification interface

290

Definition 4 (Generation-verification interface). A generation-verification interface

291

is the part of a research infrastructure that exposes the evidentiary record of each

292

substantive claim to the larger claim graph.

293

The companion paper calls the corresponding local object a verification profile [16].

294

A profile records a claim and the object that represents it. It also records the checking

295

procedure, the scope of the check, the remaining assumptions, and a stable identifier.

296

This paper leaves that claim-level taxonomy to the companion and asks how many

297

such records can be composed into a research state whose closure is derivable.

298

The interface forms a boundary between local evidence and global inference. It can

299

accept different kinds of verification profile without treating them as equivalent. For

300

each profile, it exposes the exact proposition that has been licensed. A local status can

301

then enter the graph without silently becoming a stronger claim.

302

2.4 What counts as positive evidence

303

Separating generation from verification solves a provenance problem, but it leaves a

304

semantic one. A checker can be perfectly correct about the bytes it receives while

305

those bytes encode the wrong endpoint, normalization, or theorem. The response is a

306

three-part positive-evidence obligation: identify the intended object, check an artifact,

307

and bind the two at the exact claimed scope.

308

A verification profile records how an artifact was checked. Any value, structure, or

309

theorem may later contribute evidence to the larger graph. Before that happens, the

310

graph asks for more than a successful check. The artifact must be grounded in the

311

intended research object. It must also be bound to the exact claim whose status would

312

change.

313

13

314

Definition 5 (Reference grounding). Reference grounding for a claim C is a versioned

315

specification of the object to which C refers. It records the governing definitions

316

and normalization. It records where input or reference data came from and which

317

dependencies are in use. It also records the baseline cases or calibration facts that

318

identify the intended representation.

319

Reference grounding is domain dependent. For a finite mathematical value it

320

may contain the instance family, input data, known cases, and encoding convention.

321

For a formal theorem it may contain the exact definitions, imported theory, and

322

the correspondence between the paper statement and the formal statement. For an

323

empirical object it may contain raw observations, calibration data, and the measurement

324

protocol. Each object fixes what is being checked, and the three object classes remain

325

non-interchangeable.

326

Definition 6 (Machine-checkable evidence). Machine-checkable evidence for C is a

327

versioned artifact, an independently executable checking procedure, and an acceptance

328

record produced under a declared trust and dependency policy.

329

The machine-checkable artifact may be a proof term, certificate, witness, exhaustive

330

computation, symbolic derivation, or independently rerunnable analysis. No single

331

proof assistant, solver, or certificate language is required. The selected mechanism

332

must expose an acceptance relation whose scope is adequate for C .

333

Definition 7 (Claim binding). A claim binding is an inspectable bridge showing that

334

the reference-grounded object and the machine-checked object express the exact claim C.

335

Their domain, definitions, normalization, endpoints, and declared assumptions must

336

agree.

Principle 3 (Technology-independent positive evidence). Every non-root positive claim

348

whose promotion changes reachability within a fixed root snapshot must be supported

349

by reference grounding, machine-checkable evidence, and an exact claim binding. The

350

technology realizing the machine check may be replaced. The replacement must verify

351

the same claim at no weaker scope, and it must expose its trust assumptions.

352

Definition 8 (Equivalent checking realizations). Two machine-checking realizations

353

are equivalent for a claim C when both use the same reference grounding and support

354

the same proposition. Their scope and dependency policy must also agree. In each

355

case, the evidence must be independently replayable. A stronger result is not equivalent

356

merely because it is stronger. It may replace the earlier realization only when it is

357

earned under compatible grounding and trust policy, introduces no stronger assumptions

358

or weaker scope, and carries an earned transport to C.

The infrastructure can evolve without evidentiary drift. A proof-assistant kernel

361

2.5 Keeping the mathematical object fixed

366

The positive-evidence rule is still incomplete if the identity of the checked claim can

367

drift over time. A path and a cryptographic digest identify bytes; they do not establish

368

that those bytes still describe the same mathematical object. A refreshed hash can

369

faithfully authenticate a corrupted formula, a changed endpoint, or a replacement

370

theorem that no longer implies the claim consumed downstream.

371

Definition 9 (Definition contract). A definition contract for a load-bearing mathemat-

372

ical object records the symbol, namespace, and literal meaning. It records the domain,

373

codomain, quantifier order, ranges, and endpoints. It also fixes normalization, sign or

374

orientation, occurrence multiplicity, and the transitive definitions that determine how

375

the object is used in the graph.

376

Not every project needs every field. The requirement is that every mathematically

377

material degree of freedom be fixed somewhere inspectable. For a graph theorem,

378

adjacency and endpoint conventions may be load-bearing. For an analytic estimate,

379

normalizer, weight, orientation, parameter role, and error sign may be load-bearing.

380

For a certificate, the encoder, instance, decoder, and acceptance predicate may be load-

381

bearing. Treating all three as one passing file loses the object on which the evidence

382

depends.

2.11 Verification-cost minimization

718

Principle 9 (Verification-cost minimization). Among architectures that preserve the

719

720

721

722

723

724

725

principle selects the least burdensome adequate check [16]. At the architectural level,

726

however, local choices interact. An individually cheap check may require an expensive

727

semantic bridge. It may introduce an incompatible representation. It may also force

728

every parent claim to reverify the same dependency.

729

730

2.13 Consequences for research state and publication

778

The core machinery is now in place. This subsection introduces no new graph mechanism.

779

It collects the reporting rules that follow when local evidence, composition, correction,

780

and frontier choice are considered together.

781

Principle 10 (Local positivity is not global closure). A checked local result changes

782

the status of the target only when it enters an earned edge on a complete route from

783

the admissible roots.

784

A formalized lemma, an exhaustive computation on a proper subdomain, or a

785

persuasive conditional argument therefore cannot be mistaken for the stronger claim

786

that consumes it.

787

Principle 11 (Negative evidence is bound to what it refutes). Negative evidence

788

changes only the proposition to which it is exactly bound. Refuting a route premise

789

retires that edge without refuting its proposed output. A verified counterexample to the

790

output claim instead refutes that claim at the counterexample’s stated scope.

791

A route-local failure can remain valuable when its assumptions, method, and

792

obstruction are reproducible. Other incoming edges may still establish the same output

793

claim. By contrast, a claim-bound counterexample blocks publication of that claim

794

at the refuted scope and requires any conflicting earned route to be invalidated or

795

reviewed. The binding prevents a failed method from becoming a false theorem and

796

prevents a genuine counterexample from being demoted to a mere route failure.

797

Principle 12 (Receipts are subject-relative). A machine receipt licenses only the

798

semantic subject, definition closure, artifacts, dependencies, and policy to which it was

799

bound. Byte identity alone cannot transfer the receipt to a corrected subject.

800

37

801

Principle 13 (Open obligations cannot be absorbed by description). A positive

802

composite edge must inventory every load-bearing assumption, error term, case, factor

803

opening, and transport required by its output. Naming an obligation, bounding one

804

component, or recording a conditional consumer does not discharge it.

805

This principle generalizes beyond analytic estimates. A finite certificate cannot omit

806

a residual instance. A case classification cannot omit a boundary case. A formal theorem

807

cannot hide a project axiom. A literature theorem cannot cross to a new normalization

808

by citation alone. Completeness is a typed property of the exact consumer.

809

Principle 14 (Closure-respecting publication). The evidentiary status stated in a

810

publication must be the image of an earned subgraph under a projection that preserves

811

claims, assumptions, and verification boundaries.

812

Publication may omit exploratory history, alternative routes, and internal coordina-

813

tion records, but it must not strengthen the surviving claims or erase their dependencies.

814

815

for independent implementation. An independent implementation may use different

1074

storage, agents, parsers, solvers, or proof assistants. It must still preserve the minimum

1075

loop. Reproducing only the graph vocabulary or the parallel dispatch surface would

1076

not test the method.

1077

Second, the transfer evidence is bounded but nontrivial. The three deployments

1078

address distinct mathematical questions. They instantiate two closure architectures,
50

and one pattern was reused on a third object. The method is therefore not supported

1079

by one theorem alone. Because one research program produced all three, cross-team

1080

portability remains untested. The cost objective is also qualitative: there is no universal

1081

empirical scale for verification effort. Narrower and wider parallel configurations were

1082

compared during ongoing development, but not under a fixed task suite, baseline, token

1083

budget, hardware budget, or timing protocol. The study therefore reports practical

1084

usefulness and substantial resource cost. Net speedup, duplicate-work reduction, and

1085

containment of context loss or hallucination remain unestimated. The odd-sum tree illus-

1086

trates state evolution and is excluded from the deployment count. Non-mathematical

1087

domains remain open evaluation targets. So do usability and correction behavior under

1088

independent implementations.

1089

Third, semantic completeness still depends on mathematical judgment. A definition

1090

contract exposes the fields selected by its author. It cannot prove that every material

1091

field was selected. Root admission may also misclassify an unsupported claim as an

1092

assumption or imported result. Required tokens and source anchors detect known

1093

corruption modes, not meaning itself. An assumption or error inventory may still omit

1094

an obligation. Hostile review and independent formalization therefore remain essential.

1095

The method contributes a narrower guarantee: once a defect is found and the affected

1096

receipts are identified, the correction protocol revokes their current inferential status

1097

instead of leaving them active behind a prose-only warning.

1098

6 Conclusions

1099

A proof engine begins where candidate generation ends. It fixes the exact target and

1100

admissible roots, then decomposes that consumer into typed claim paths. It records

1101

what each local artifact establishes. Reference grounding, an adequate check, and

1102

exact claim binding create candidate evidence. All-or-nothing promotion and graph

1103

composition determine whether that evidence may reach the target. Formal proofs,

1104

51

1105

certificates, direct witnesses, and replicated computations are different edge boundaries.

1106

They are chosen for the mathematical object and retain non-interchangeable evidentiary

1107

meanings.

1108

The same graph supplies an agent-to-claim control plane across workers and time.

1109

Typed work units allow independent obligations to proceed concurrently. Asynchronous

1110

returns enter through receipts; worker authority cannot substitute for them. Within a

1111

fixed semantic snapshot, valid evidence accumulates. After a correction, affected edges

1112

lose current reachability, historical receipts remain auditable, and re-earning requires

1113

a fresh check bound to the corrected object. Frontier discipline then keeps new work

1114

attached to the exact unresolved consumer.

1115

The completed cases show that substantially different proof shapes can satisfy

1116

these rules. The mathematical construction, its kernel endpoints, and exact paper-to-

1117

kernel binding close the Hamilton classification. Structural mathematics, exhaustive

1118

certificates, semantic checking, and kernel replay close Erdős Problem 848 for every

1119

N . The rational-Dyck project reuses direct closure on a third object. The odd-sum

1120

example displays the same logic without borrowing the known truth of its target.

1121

The even-scope theorem is retained. The false shortcut retires. The precise recurrence

1122

frontier stays open.

1123

Operational use shows that the graph can coordinate useful concurrent research,

1124

while wide dispatch can impose substantial token and communication costs. Proof

1125

Engine 2.0 will therefore study reservations, typed handoffs, receipt-only inter-agent

1126

trust, conflict resolution, scheduling, and resource accounting on matched work units.

1127

Until those evaluations exist, this article contributes the fail-closed state method. A

1128

publication should make no stronger claim than the earned graph supports. Its status

1129

should change when its evidence changes.
