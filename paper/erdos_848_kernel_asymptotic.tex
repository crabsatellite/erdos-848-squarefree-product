\begin{abstract}
For $N\geq1$, let $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ and suppose that $ab+1$
is not squarefree for every $a,b\in A$.  Sawhney proved that, for all
sufficiently large $N$, the largest possible cardinality of $A$ is the
number of integers congruent to $7$ modulo $25$.  We give a Lean~4
formalization of this asymptotic theorem and a short paper-to-kernel map.
The literal interval statement is obtained from the formalization's
zero-based range convention by a kernel-checked indexing lemma.  We also
  kernel-check two exact small instances, the exact online-colouring trace
  through $N=5{,}000{,}000$, and the precise finite-reduction statement.

This note does not claim a complete all-$N$ resolution of Erd\H{o}s
Problem~848; the remaining tail $N\geq5{,}000{,}000$ is not yet closed.
Every
numbered mathematical result asserted here is mapped to a declaration whose
Lean axiom audit contains only \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the sharp constructions}

An integer is squarefree when no prime square divides it.  Write
\[
 [N]=\{1,\ldots,N\},\qquad
 \CN rN=\{n\in[N]:n\equiv r\pmod {25}\}.
\]

\begin{definition}\label{def:admissible}
A finite set $A\subseteq[N]$ is \emph{848-admissible} if
\[
  ab+1\ \text{is not squarefree for every }a,b\in A.
\]
Let $P(N)$ denote the statement that every 848-admissible
$A\subseteq[N]$ satisfies $|A|\leq|\CN 7N|$.
\end{definition}

In Lean these are \leanname{NonSquarefreeProductProp} and
\leanname{OriginalProblem848Statement}.  Repeated elements are allowed in
the quantified pair, so the diagonal condition $a^2+1$ is part of the
hypothesis.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:base-constructions}
Both $\CN 7N$ and $\CN {18}N$ are 848-admissible.  Moreover
$|\CN {18}N|\leq|\CN 7N|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $a,b$ lie in the same one of the two displayed classes, then
\[
 ab+1\equiv7^2+1\equiv18^2+1\equiv0\pmod {25}.
\]
Thus $5^2\mid ab+1$.  The cardinality comparison is the elementary count of
the two residue classes in $[N]$.  The formal construction theorems are
\leanname{A7_has_property_public} and
\leanname{A18_has_property_public}; the exact range counts are
\leanname{A7_card_public}, \leanname{A18_card_public}, and
\leanname{A18_card_le_A7_public}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound $|A|\geq|\CN 7N|$ is therefore immediate.  The content of the
problem is the matching upper bound.

\section{The stability theorem}

The formalization follows Sawhney's stability route~\cite{Sawhney848}.
It first uses the diagonal condition to constrain each element, then uses
off-diagonal products to rule out a dense mixture of incompatible residue
patterns.

For compatibility with Mathlib, the large source file initially works in
\texttt{Finset.range N}, namely $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$.  Put
\[
 A_7(N)=\{n<N:n\equiv7\pmod {25}\},\qquad
 A_{18}(N)=\{n<N:n\equiv18\pmod {25}\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Formalized stability theorem]\label{thm:stability}
There exist $\eta>0$ and $N_0\in\NN$ such that $\eta<1/25$ and, for every
$N\geq N_0$, every 848-admissible $A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ satisfying
\[
 |A|\geq(1/25-\eta)N
\]
is contained in either $A_7(N)$ or $A_{18}(N)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the proposition \leanname{SawhneyMainAt}, instantiated by the
kernel-checked theorem \leanname{sawhney_main}.  The formal proof chooses
$\eta=1/2000$ and a non-explicit threshold large enough for its prime-counting
error.  Its main ingredients are as follows.

First, if $p^2\mid a^2+1$, then $p\equiv1\pmod4$, and the congruence has two
roots modulo $p^2$.  This supplies the diagonal sieve.  Second, mixed
$7$- and $18$-classes force a prime-square witness other than $5^2$ in their
cross-products.  Counts in fixed classes modulo $25p^2$, $50p^2$, and
$100p^2$ give the off-diagonal sieve.  Third, the proof evaluates the finite
reciprocal-square contribution through the fixed cutoff $2000$ and bounds
the remaining tail.  A prime-counting $o(N)$ estimate absorbs the additive
errors after increasing $N_0$.  Finally, an exhaustive parity and residue
case split shows that any set above the stated density threshold is contained
in one of the two base classes.

These steps are declarations in \texttt{Asymptotic.lean}; no numerical
output is imported as an axiom or trusted certificate.
\end{proof}

\section{The asymptotic endpoint}

\begin{theorem}[Range-indexed endpoint]\label{thm:range-asymptotic}
There exists $N_0$ such that, for every $N\geq N_0$ and every
848-admissible $A\subseteq\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$,
\[
 |A|\leq|A_7(N)|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $|A|>|A_7(N)|$.  The exact residue-class count implies
$|A|\geq(1/25-\eta)N$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} then puts $A$ inside
$A_7(N)$ or $A_{18}(N)$.  In the first case cardinality is immediate; in the
second it follows from $|A_{18}(N)|\leq|A_7(N)|$.  In Lean this deduction is
\leanname{problem_848_large_of_sawhney}, and the unconditional existential
endpoint is \leanname{problem_848_asymptotic}.  The fixed-$N$ proposition is
\leanname{Problem848Statement}.
\end{proof}

The zero-based convention must be bridged explicitly rather than dismissed
as asymptotically harmless.

\begin{lemma}[Index bridge]\label{lem:index-bridge}
For every $N$,
\[
 \CN 7N=A_7(N+1).
\]
Consequently the range-indexed statement at $N+1$ implies $P(N)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two sets have the same upper bound.  Their only possible difference is
$0$, which is not congruent to $7$ modulo $25$.  The corresponding Lean
declarations are \leanname{originalA7_eq_range} and
\leanname{original_of_range_succ}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Literal asymptotic endpoint]\label{thm:original-asymptotic}
There exists $N_0$ such that $P(N)$ holds for every $N\geq N_0$.
Equivalently, for all sufficiently large $N$, the exact maximum is
$|\CN 7N|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:range-asymptotic} at $N+1$ and then
Lemma~\ref{lem:index-bridge}.  The construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:base-constructions} supplies equality.  This is
\leanname{erdos848_original_asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite reduction and the certified prefix}

The formal source also contains direct finite checks at range sizes $50$ and
$100$.  After applying the index bridge, they become the following literal
instances.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:small-instances}
The statements $P(49)$ and $P(99)$ hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kernel-checked declarations are \leanname{erdos848_original_N49} and
\leanname{erdos848_original_N99}.  Their upstream range-indexed proofs reduce
the diagonal candidates to explicit finite sets and exclude oversized
admissible subsets by \texttt{decide}; they do not use
\texttt{native\_decide}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact finite reduction]\label{cor:finite-reduction}
There exists an integer $N_0$ such that $P(N)$ holds for every $N\geq N_0$
and
\[
  \bigl(\forall N,\ P(N)\bigr)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \bigl(\forall N<N_0,\ P(N)\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose $N_0$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:original-asymptotic}.  The forward
direction is restriction.  Conversely, use the assumed finite prefix below
$N_0$ and the asymptotic theorem at or above $N_0$.  The Lean declaration is
\leanname{erdos848_finite_reduction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Online-colouring soundness]
\label{prop:prefix-colouring-soundness}
Fix $N$.  Suppose that every $x\in[N]$ for which $x^2+1$ is nonsquarefree is
assigned a colour $c_N(x)\in\CN 7N$, and suppose that distinct diagonal
candidates of the same colour satisfy that $xy+1$ is squarefree.  Then $P(N)$
holds.  Consequently, a certificate providing such a colouring separately at
every prefix through $L$ proves $P(N)$ for every $1\leq N\leq L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Restrict $c_N$ to an 848-admissible set $A$.  Two distinct members of $A$
cannot have the same colour, since their cross-product plus one is required to
be nonsquarefree.  Thus $c_N|_A$ is an injection into $\CN 7N$.  The Lean
interface is \leanname{PrefixColouringState}; its soundness declarations are
\leanname{originalProblem_of_prefixColouringState} and
\leanname{originalProblem_prefix_of_colouringCertificate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel-checked prefix]
\label{thm:prefix-close}
The statement $P(N)$ holds for every $N\leq5{,}000{,}000$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The official payload records $525{,}808$ diagonal vertices: $200{,}000$
residue-$7$ anchors, $200{,}000$ residue-$18$ candidates, and $125{,}808$
outside-base candidates.  It records the interval history of every anchor and
a covering reverse-assignment history for every non-anchor candidate.  Each
assignment identifies one of the three occupant roles at an anchor.  The
checker verifies that the histories cover every prefix, that equal-colour
distinct occupants have a squarefree cross-product plus one, and that every
nonsquarefree diagonal candidate occurs in the indexed sequence.  Primality
and squarefreeness facts are discharged by the split five-million Pratt and
factor certificates.  Applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:prefix-colouring-soundness} yields the Lean theorem
\leanname{erdos848_through_five_million}.  The older range endpoints
\leanname{erdos848_prefix_close}, \leanname{erdos848_first_low_close}, and
\leanname{erdos848_second_low_close} are direct projections of this single
certificate, not additional assumptions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact remaining-tail assembly]
\label{prop:five-million-tail-assembly}
Suppose $P(N)$ is proved for every $N\geq5{,}000{,}000$.  Then $P(N)$ holds
for every $N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix-close} below the cut and the assumed tail theorem
at or above it.  Lean checks this literal assembly as
\leanname{erdos848_full_of_five_million_tail}.  This proposition does not
assert the tail premise; it isolates the only remaining all-$N$ obligation.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-reduction} is a logical reduction, not by itself a
completed finite computation.  The threshold produced inside the formal proof is
non-explicit because it is chosen after an eventual prime-counting bound.
The current public problem record accordingly describes Problem~848 as
``resolved up to a finite check''~\cite{Erdos848Page}.  The explicit range
through five million is now certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix-close}.
No unchecked exploratory finite-search output is used by any theorem in this
repository.

The earlier diagonal and Hall reductions are retained as independently
auditable structural layers.  In particular, the generated diagonal
certificate proves that every outside-base $x\leq5{,}000{,}000$ with
nonsquarefree $x^2+1$ occurs in an exact sorted marker, checks its odd and two
odd mod-$4$ projections, and proves the rational count envelopes consumed by
the Hall argument.  The kernel endpoints include
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\leanname{GeneratedLowDiagonalCoverage.certificateContainsOfNotSquarefree}\\[-2pt]
\leanname{GeneratedLowDiagonalCoverage.firstLowDiagonalBounds}\\[-2pt]
\leanname{GeneratedLowDiagonalCoverage.secondLowDiagonalBounds}
\end{center}
These layers are no longer open premises for the published finite range: the
single exact trace certificate proves the complete statement through five
million directly.

\begin{remark}[Boundary of the earlier all-$N$ claim]
An earlier exploratory draft assembled an all-$N$ claim from an online finite
colouring and several analytic block programs.  The complete online colouring
through $5{,}000{,}000$ has now been lifted to the kernel-checked payload used
in Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix-close}.  The final
$N\geq5{,}000{,}000$ argument remains a separate obligation.  No earlier
program output is treated as a proof of that remaining statement.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

The public mathematical closure established here consists of the asymptotic
theorem and the exact range $N\leq5{,}000{,}000$.  The conjectured
$7\pmod {25}$ cardinality is therefore kernel-checked on that prefix and for
every sufficiently large $N$.  A complete all-$N$ solution still requires a
kernel proof of the final tail in
Proposition~\ref{prop:five-million-tail-assembly}; until that premise is supplied,
this work should not be reported as a full resolution of Erd\H{o}s Problem~848.
